{\LARGE \fontfamily{bch}\fontseries{eb}\selectfont \setlength{\leftmargini}{3pt}\begin{description}
\item[So Many Publications] {\labelsp} For most topics, too many peer-reviewed papers for 
researchers to consider each one individually.  Can Text Mining and/or Natural Language Processing tools help?    
\vspace{8pt}
\item[Modeling Rhetoric] {\labelsp} The most useful papers for a researchers will 
typically not only be \txtit{about} some topic.  They will, more specifically, 
\txtit{endorse}, \txtit{critique}, or neutrally \txtit{evaluate} an argument or theory 
\vspace{8pt}
\item[Thematic Connections] {\labelsp} If a paper cites another, what discursive role does this 
reference serve?  To support a claim the current author makes or repeats from elsewhere; 
to indicate an influential precedent in the field; to identify an early example where 
a theory or terminology was introduced; to direct readers toward a lengthier or 
more historically significant discussion of a topic;
 to single out the referenced work as exceptionally clear, influential, 
or innovative; or to initiate a critical analysis of the work?  
\vspace{8pt}
\item[Across Disciplines] {\labelsp} Computational tools might discover connections 
between subject-areas that researchers miss on their own  
\end{description}}\end{minipage}
