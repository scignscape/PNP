
\section{Transparency}\fbeg{}\begin{frame}{\ft{Data Transparency Becomes a Priority}}


\begin{ftxt}

Some publishers have started to \textsl{require} authors to 
provide raw data alongside their articles, or else 
to explicitly state why they are unable to do 
so (assuming their research involves empirical data 
sets to begin with).  Similar policies have been 
adopted by funding agencies.  For example, the Bill and 
Melinda Gates foundation requires that all grantees 
\q{\textcolor[RGB]{27, 31, 130}{include a Data Availability Statement (DAS) describing
where the underlying data can be found ... 
Underlying data includes all primary data, metadata, and
any additional information needed to understand, assess,
and replicate the study findings}.} \\\vspace*{-23pt}

\href{https://openaccess.gatesfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Grantee-DAS-Guide.pdf}%
{\textcolor[RGB]{130, 27, 83}{Gates Foundation 2025 Open Access Policy: Data Availability Guide}}%

\end{ftxt}

\end{frame}
